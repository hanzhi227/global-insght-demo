# SAF-002 — Coolant CL-7 Chemical Spill Response

Fictional demo document — not approved for real plant operations.

**Company:** Northstar Precision Components — Cedar Falls Plant  
**Effective date:** 2026-09-01  
**Revision:** 3  
**Owner role:** Plant Safety Lead  
**Approval role:** Plant Manager

## 1. Purpose and response boundary

This document governs response authorization for coolant CL-7 released at the Cedar Falls Plant during AX-210 aluminum mounting bracket production. It covers floor spills, transfer-hose leakage, damaged containers, and coolant carried toward conveyor C-12. CL-7 is a fictional water-miscible machining coolant; its current controlled safety data sheet governs chemical-specific protection and incompatibilities. Never infer composition or safe handling from this procedure alone. An unavailable or inconsistent safety data sheet prevents routine cleanup authorization.

Safety owns incident classification, cleanup authorization, exposure control, and area reopening. Maintenance owns equipment checks and leak-source repairs. Operations owns scheduling and material flow. Quality alone releases held product. Never bypass guards, restart isolated equipment without authorized release, falsify records, or release held product outside Quality authority. C-12 intervention follows SAF-001; contractor participation and hot work follow SAF-003.

## 2. Detect, withdraw, and report

Anyone finding a release stops their immediate task, warns nearby workers, and reports the location, apparent source, visible extent, and injuries to the Operations Shift Lead and Safety Duty Officer. Do not walk through coolant to estimate volume or rescue tools. Stop normal pedestrian movement through the affected aisle. If someone is exposed, activate the site emergency response and follow the current safety data sheet and emergency team's instructions; do not delay assistance for incident paperwork.

Operations pauses affected material movement and records container positions, bracket lot identifiers, and the time of discovery. Keep AX-210 brackets potentially contacted by coolant, absorbent, splash, or uncontrolled handling on hold for Quality assessment. A clean-looking surface is not evidence of product acceptability. People may activate accessible emergency stops from a safe location, but reaching through a guard or entering a danger zone requires SAF-001 isolation.

## 3. Classification and measurable limits

Routine cleanup is eligible only when all conditions are true: the liquid is positively identified as CL-7; estimated volume is no more than 5 liters; affected floor area is no more than 2 square meters; there is no injury, visible mist, smoke, unknown chemical mixing, or electrical contact; and the release is at least 3 meters from an unprotected drain. The source must be stopped or safely contained without entering an equipment danger zone. Two trained spill responders and a serviceable spill kit must be available. These are fictional internal limits, not general chemical safety guidance.

Any failed condition makes the event an escalated spill. Establish an initial exclusion boundary at least 5 meters from the visible spill edge and withdraw to a safe route. Safety may enlarge the boundary for migration, ventilation, or adjacent hazards; workers may not reduce it themselves. Report any drain entry, continuing discharge, uncertain identity, or exposure immediately to the Plant Safety Lead. That role activates the site emergency response and determines external notification needs without waiting for a final volume estimate.

## 4. Authorized routine cleanup

Safety records the classification and authorizes named responder roles before cleanup. Responders review the current CL-7 safety data sheet, select its prescribed protective equipment, inspect their kit, and confirm unobstructed withdrawal routes. Do not improvise respirators or attempt cleanup in an atmosphere requiring respiratory protection; escalate instead.

From the clean side, place compatible absorbent barriers to limit spread, protect drain approaches without entering the spill, and collect free liquid using the kit's approved method. Do not hose coolant into drains, use compressed air to disperse liquid, or mix neutralizers into CL-7. Bag or containerize spent absorbent and disposable protection in compatible labeled waste containers. Label the contents, incident number, collection date, and provisional waste status. Safety arranges characterization and approved disposal; ordinary trash is not an assumed route.

Maintenance addresses the hose, fitting, reservoir, or other source under the applicable equipment procedure. If C-12 is isolated, keep its boundary and locks intact throughout cleanup. Clean only surfaces within the authorized scope. Discovery of liquid beneath energized equipment, inside guarded machinery, or beyond the estimated footprint stops routine cleanup and requires reclassification.

## 5. Inspection and reopening

Responders report collected waste, residual staining, and inaccessible areas. Safety and Operations inspect the affected floor in adequate lighting after cleanup and again after 15 minutes. Both inspections must find no visible free liquid, active seepage, or contaminated pedestrian route before Safety reopens the area. Persistent residue or a slippery surface requires additional treatment and renewed inspection; absence of odor is not an acceptance test.

Maintenance documents equipment condition and repair completion, but cannot reopen the area. Safety reopening does not authorize restart of isolated C-12: SAF-001 release remains mandatory. Operations restores scheduling and traffic only after Safety reopening and applicable equipment release. Quality separately decides disposition of every held lot, including brackets moved during containment. Hot work remains prohibited within an active spill boundary and requires SAF-003 after reopening.

## 6. Exceptions, escalation, and incident record

A missing responder, depleted kit, blocked exit, or uncertain protective equipment cancels routine eligibility. Secure the boundary and escalate rather than borrowing unverified materials. If the incident outlasts a shift, Safety transfers the boundary, classification, waste status, and unresolved hazards to the incoming Safety Duty Officer before relief.

Retain discovery report, classification basis, safety data sheet revision used, cleanup authorization, waste records, both inspection times, reopening approval, and Quality hold references for 3 years in the controlled Safety repository. Record estimates as estimates; amend errors without hiding original entries. The Plant Safety Lead reviews recurring leaks with Maintenance and Operations.

## 7. Revision history

Revision 1 established spill reporting. Revision 2 separated product disposition from area reopening. Revision 3, effective 2026-09-01, defines routine eligibility, escalated boundaries, dual inspections, and waste traceability.

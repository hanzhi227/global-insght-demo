# MNT-001 — C-12 Preventive Maintenance and Fault Diagnosis

Fictional demo document — not approved for real plant operations.

**Company:** Northstar Precision Components, Cedar Falls Plant
**Effective date:** 2026-09-01 | **Revision:** 3
**Owner:** Maintenance Supervisor | **Approval role:** Plant Engineering Manager
**Controlled scope:** Conveyor C-12 serving AX-210 aluminum mounting bracket production.

## 1. Purpose and responsibility

This instruction controls conveyor condition checks, preventive maintenance, and diagnosis of intermittent transfer faults. Maintenance owns equipment checks and repair evidence. Operations owns scheduling, accumulation management, and material flow. Safety owns isolation authorization, permits, and authorized release from isolation. Quality alone owns release of held product. Coordinate those decisions through SAF-001, OPS-001, and QUA-001 respectively; this instruction does not reproduce their numerical rules. No bypassing guards, no restarting isolated equipment without authorized release, and no falsifying records are permitted.

The Maintenance Supervisor assigns a technician and confirms the scheduled window with Operations. A shift operator may report symptoms and read external indicators but may not perform maintenance adjustments. The technician identifies the drive-end designation before recording measurements; left and right mean viewed in the normal direction of travel.

## 2. Service intervals and equipment baseline

Perform an external running observation each production shift. Complete isolated mechanical checks every 250 operating hours or 30 calendar days, whichever comes first. Inspect the drive gearbox oil and motor connections every 1,000 operating hours or 90 days, whichever comes first. A missed interval makes the task overdue; it does not create automatic permission to continue operation. The supervisor requests a scheduling decision from Operations and records the equipment disposition.

The unloaded reference belt speed is 0.30 m/s, acceptable from 0.28 to 0.32 m/s. Measure travel across a marked 3.0 m section using a stopwatch, without reaching through guarding. Belt edge clearance must remain at least 8 mm from each fixed side member. On isolated equipment, belt deflection at the marked return-span midpoint must be 10–14 mm under a 20 N test force. Drive bearing surface temperature during the standard unloaded observation must not exceed 65°C. These are maintenance limits, not product acceptance limits.

## 3. Preparation and preventive work

1. Obtain the work order, current equipment drawing, approved replacement-part list, and previous three service records. Ask Operations to clear brackets and identify any accumulation that cannot be moved. Do not treat an empty belt as proof of safe isolation.
2. Request Safety-controlled isolation under SAF-001. Verify the documented authorization before opening guards or approaching stored belt tension. Record the authorization reference in the work order.
3. Remove loose chips with an approved vacuum. Collect oily debris separately. CL-7 coolant residue may indicate carryover from adjacent machining; report the source rather than washing it into floor drains.
4. Inspect belt seams for lifting, cord exposure, or edge separation. Any exposed reinforcing cord, missing splice fastener, or separation longer than 15 mm requires repair before maintenance clearance. Record the defect location relative to the marked splice.
5. Check rollers for free rotation while isolated. Replace a roller that binds, has a cracked shell, or has radial play exceeding 0.5 mm. Measure play with a dial indicator, not by descriptive estimates alone.
6. Measure belt deflection and side clearance. Adjust only by the equipment procedure, moving matched take-up screws equally before making a documented tracking correction. Record both initial and final readings.
7. Inspect gearbox level at the sight glass with equipment stationary. Accept oil between the two engraved marks. Use only the lubricant listed on the equipment bill of materials; unknown oil requires supervisor review. Refit guards and account for tools before requesting release.

## 4. Fault diagnosis and controlled trial

For a drift or mistracking complaint, compare left and right clearances, roller contamination, and take-up positions. Do not compensate for a seized roller by increasing tension. For hesitation or surge, compare observed speed with the baseline, inspect belt slip evidence, and review drive fault timestamps. A stalled conveyor with a humming motor requires immediate stop and Safety isolation, not repeated reset attempts. For false accumulation signals, clean the external sensor window only when safely accessible; internal alignment work requires isolation.

After authorized release, Operations controls an unloaded five-minute trial. Maintenance observes speed, tracking, abnormal noise, and bearing temperature from outside guards. Stop the trial if any listed limit fails, a guard is insecure, or vibration visibly moves a mounting foot. A successful trial permits maintenance clearance only; Operations decides material flow and Quality decides product disposition.

## 5. Escalation, records, and exceptions

Escalate recurring drift after two documented adjustments, an unreadable equipment label, or an unavailable specified replacement to the Maintenance Supervisor. Do not fabricate substitutes or defeat a sensor to maintain throughput. If a calibrated force gauge or dial indicator is unavailable, leave the affected check incomplete and record equipment status; visual judgment cannot replace a required measurement.

The work order records meter hours, symptoms, authorization and release references, instrument identifiers, before-and-after values, parts used, trial results, and technician role acknowledgement. Link suspected bracket damage to the Quality hold reference without releasing that product. Record unavailable information as unavailable, never as a passing value.

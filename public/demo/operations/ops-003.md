# OPS-003 — Material Staging and Shipment Release Coordination

**Northstar Precision Components — Cedar Falls Plant**  
**Effective date:** 2026-09-01 | **Revision:** 3  
**Owner:** Materials and Logistics Manager | **Approval role:** Plant Manager  
**Category:** Operations | **Controlled record:** OPS-003-F1 Staging and Dispatch Checklist

**Fictional demo document — not approved for real plant operations.**

## 1. Purpose and authority

This procedure controls staging locations, production replenishment, shipment preparation, and dispatch coordination for AX-210 aluminum mounting brackets at Northstar Precision Components, Cedar Falls Plant. “Shipment release” in this title means Operations' dispatch decision after required approvals exist; it does not mean product acceptance. Quality alone owns product release under QUA-001.

Operations owns scheduling and material flow. Safety owns isolation authorization and permits under SAF-001. Maintenance owns equipment checks under MNT-001, including concerns involving conveyor C-12, hydraulic press HP-4, or air dryer AD-2. Product or handling concerns involving coolant CL-7 are referred to the applicable owning function, not resolved by a logistics expediter.

Never bypass guards, restart isolated equipment without authorized release, falsify records, or release held product without Quality authorization. Neither a waiting truck nor an urgent customer order permits an exception to these invariants.

## 2. Roles and location controls

The Material Coordinator controls the staging register and replenishment sequence. The Shift Lead requests material against the active dispatch schedule. The Shipping Coordinator prepares shipment documents and verifies dispatch readiness. Quality provides the release reference. The Operations Manager resolves competing production and shipment priorities.

Use four identified location classes: IN for incoming material awaiting its required status decision; READY for material authorized for production use; HOLD for segregated material awaiting disposition; and OUT for released product assigned to a shipment. Each physical slot has a unique location identifier recorded in the staging register.

A slot may contain material from only one batch or incoming material lot. HOLD locations must remain distinct from READY and OUT. Do not temporarily place held product in a released slot, even when its container label appears clear. A vacant slot is not available until its previous contents and location record have been reconciled.

## 3. Production staging and replenishment

Stage only the active batch and one identified next batch in the production-side queue. Keep the next batch separate and unopened until the transition authorized through OPS-002. Additional material remains in its assigned storage location. This two-batch queue limit prevents space pressure from turning into an undocumented mixed-batch changeover.

The Shift Lead requests replenishment when the active batch has 30 minutes of planned material coverage remaining. Calculate coverage from the current approved dispatch rate and confirmed available quantity, not from an assumed full container. The Material Coordinator acknowledges the request within 10 minutes and reports any shortage to the lead.

If replenishment cannot support the forecast, follow OPS-001 escalation. Operations may resequence approved work but may not substitute an unidentified lot or consume material whose status is uncertain. Container capacity, batch identity, and the movement-record timing remain governed by OPS-002.

## 4. Stage finished product for shipment

The Shipping Coordinator builds a shipment list using the order reference, required quantity, approved destination, container identifiers, batch identifiers, and Quality release references. Confirm the release covers the actual containers selected. A release for another batch, an earlier quantity, or an unspecified group is not sufficient evidence.

Move product into OUT only after its release status is confirmed. Record the location transfer under OPS-002. For orders drawing from multiple batches, list each batch separately and preserve container identity. Do not create a mixed-batch production container to simplify picking.

Compare the sum of container quantities with the shipment list. The permitted unexplained shipment quantity difference is zero. An approved partial shipment must have a revised shipment quantity and order reference before dispatch; a handwritten promise to send the balance later is not approval.

## 5. Dispatch readiness gate

Complete the dispatch checklist at least 60 minutes before planned carrier pickup. Verify order reference, destination, container count, actual piece quantity, batch identity, product release evidence, and transport-document consistency. Confirm packaging acceptance through the applicable Quality requirements without reproducing their technical limits here.

A second qualified Operations or Shipping role independently compares physical container identifiers and quantities with the shipment list. Record both verification roles and completion times. The Shipping Coordinator may authorize dispatch only after every required field is complete and all exceptions are resolved.

If the gate is incomplete at the 60-minute point, notify the Operations Manager and revise the pickup forecast. The carrier may wait in an approved area, but product remains under plant control. No truck-loading instruction overrides a hold or unresolved identity discrepancy.

## 6. Exceptions and status changes

If Quality imposes a hold after OUT staging, stop dispatch, identify affected containers, move them to HOLD through a documented transfer, and revise the shipment list. If the load has already departed, immediately notify Quality and the Operations Manager, preserve carrier and shipment references, and follow Quality's containment direction.

For damaged packaging, missing labels, or mismatched counts, segregate the affected containers and seek the owning function's disposition. Do not relabel uncertain product from memory. During system outages, use controlled paper records with actual event times; reconcile them before final dispatch authorization.

## 7. Records, measures, and revision history

Retain the staging register, replenishment requests, dispatch checklist, independent verification, shipment list, release references, and carrier handoff record with the shipment record set. Weekly targets are 100% shipments with linked Quality release evidence, zero unexplained quantity differences, and 100% readiness gates completed or formally escalated at the 60-minute point.
